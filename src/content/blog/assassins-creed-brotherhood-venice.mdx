I've had this game for a few years already, and I must have played the four tutorial missions four times already, and the first proper mission once or twice, but I've never managed to get further, which is really a shame because I really like what I've already seen of it! I did the last run of the start of the campaign a few days ago, and this time I aim to go further. We're finally going to see the end of the campaign! 

The short review after a few games would be: a fun puzzle-solving cooperative game that does respect the Assassin's Creed vibe and theme quite a lot. Good narrative theme and exciting surprises every time you start a mission.

Let's now hop up to a longer overview.

--- 

It's an Assassin's Creed game, so obviously the main mechanic is stealth. Characters can be *incognito* or *exposed*. When you are incognito, enemies do not detect you by default, and you can use your hidden blade to get easy kills, and you can move around the map easily and avoid occupied areas. When you're exposed, some of your skills and tools are not available, enemies will hunt you down, and you cannot complete certain objectives. 

On top of that, when you get *exposed*, the *alarm* is raised, which increases the number of enemies spawning every turn. There are a few ways to stop the *alarm*, but this will cost time and resources. 

While incognito, you make a *detection test* when an enemy enters your tile, or when you enter a tile with enemies. If you fail, you become *exposed*.

Every turn, each Assassin gets 3 action points, and you can play them in no particular order, so you can start a turn with one Assassin, then move to the second one, and go back to the first one if you have cubes remaining. This makes the game very puzzly and cooperative, because the optimal play will often make you switch from one Assassin to the other to make combined actions to accomplish your goal. We combined three Assassins by using an item from the first one and the passive from the second to allow the third to save one of them that would have almost certainly died without help and it was soooo satisfying! 

You use your action points to use some of your items, to fight with your hidden blade or with a less subtle weapon (I'm not a very sneaky Assassin), to hide bodies and to accomplish objectives. When fighting overtly, you take the risk of losing some hit points if you don't manage to kill every opponent (on top of getting *exposed*), but you might be able to take out a larger group of opponents than if you were doing it in stealth. 

Once the Assassins are done, enemies spawn, hunt down *exposed* Assassins or move randomly and then attack available targets. Even in the first few games, we thought that it was quite easy to lose a lot of hit points in a single turn, especially once the alarm gets raised.

On some maps, there is a tower you can climb to synchronize the area, revealing new enemies and chests to earn more loot. It's very thematic and quite fun.

For every quest, you open an envelope and discover a few new cards. These include event cards that bring a small modifier to a turn, new equipment to obtain when looting opponents, or rarer loot cards that will force you to get to the chests on the map.

Between missions, you get a few choices to unlock a few other perks, like healing wounded Assassins or crafting new items.

I've backed the game on Kickstarter, which means that I have tons of miniatures with it, probably too many, to be honest. But playing on the table with painted scenery, a tower, and some paper houses I printed and folded together to get 3D buildings to represent the heights is an incredible experience. I'll get some pictures from the next game, I promise. 

On the rest of the miniatures, there are obviously the Assassins, a few apprentices, and a shit ton of enemy minis. I might try to put some paint on those once I'm done with the current painting project, but I doubt it'd get done this year.

Obviously, still with the objective of improving the player experience, I recommend playing with the soundtrack of Assassin's Creed II in the background.

That's it for the review. I'll write another one once I'm further in the game, with a few pictures and maybe some painted miniatures. In the meantime, have fun!
